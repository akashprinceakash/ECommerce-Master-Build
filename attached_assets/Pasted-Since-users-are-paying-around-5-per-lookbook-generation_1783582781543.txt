Since users are paying **around $5 per lookbook generation**, the experience needs to feel premium and maximize the chance of a successful, realistic output on the first try. Looking at your current Kasha Lookbook UI and comparing it with IDM-VTON, I would make several changes.

## 1. Allow users to upload their own photo (Highly Recommended) ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

This is the biggest improvement.

Instead of always using a fixed AI model, let users choose:

```
Choose Model

○ Use AI Model (Quick)
● Upload My Photo (Best Results)
```

Benefits:

* Much more personal.
* Higher customer satisfaction.
* Customers are more willing to pay.
* Better conversion to purchase.

This is exactly why Hugging Face's IDM-VTON asks for a human image.

## Overall Recommendation

If Kasha is charging about **$5 per generation**, I would definitely redesign the Lookbook around the user's own photo rather than a fixed mannequin or AI avatar. Users paying that amount expect to see **themselves** wearing the outfit, not just a generic model.

I would also move away from a text-heavy interface to a more visual, guided workflow. Large product thumbnails, photo-quality checks, a preview before generation, a clear cost confirmation, progress updates, and multiple output images will make the experience feel much more premium and reduce failed generations.

Finally, I noticed your product images (second screenshot) are marketing renders. For AI virtual try-on, you'll get significantly better accuracy if you provide the model with clean, front-facing garment images on a plain or transparent background, similar to the garment examples used by IDM-VTON. This change alone can noticeably improve the realism and consistency of the generated results.
