From a **lookbook/user experience** perspective, this is a good first step, but **I wouldn't consider it production-ready yet**. Here are the main issues I notice:

### 👍 What's working

* The shirt and trousers are displayed together.
* The left panel with "My Saved Pieces" is intuitive.
* The large canvas gives users space to create outfits.
* The "Save Look" functionality fits the purpose of a lookbook.

### ❌ What needs improvement

#### 1. The garments are floating independently

The polo shirt and trousers don't look like they're being worn by a person or mannequin. They appear as two separate product images placed on the canvas.

A lookbook should make users visualize the complete outfit.

**Better options:**

* Use an invisible mannequin/body proportions so garments align naturally.
* Even better (as you mentioned previously), use a male/female/kids silhouette where garments snap onto the body.

---

#### 2. Alignment

The trousers are not centered under the shirt.

They should automatically align:

* shirt centered over waist
* pants directly below shirt
* proper spacing

The user shouldn't have to manually position basic outfit pieces.

---

#### 3. Scale

The shirt is much larger than the trousers.

Every garment should have predefined scaling.

Example:

```
Polo
   │
 Waist
   │
Trouser
```

instead of

```
POLOOOOO

 tiny pant
```

---

#### 4. Empty canvas

Nearly 80% of the canvas is empty.

Possible improvements:

* Light mannequin outline
* Golf course silhouette
* Neutral fashion background
* Grid/guides while dragging

---

#### 5. Snapping

Dragging should snap garments into predefined positions.

Example:

```
Head

Shirt
 ↓ snaps

Pant
 ↓ snaps

Shoes
```

Not free-floating everywhere.

---

#### 6. Outfit naming

"My Outfit" is good, but consider auto-generating names like:

* Weekend Golf
* Club Uniform
* Summer Polo Look

Users can still edit it.

---

## My recommendation (best for Ka.Sha)

Since your brand is focused on **custom apparel**, I'd move away from a blank canvas.

Instead:

```
           Male

       [Polo]

      [Trouser]

      [Shoes]


Female

Kids
```

When the user clicks **Men**, show a male silhouette.

When they drag a polo:

* it automatically fits the torso.

When they drag trousers:

* they automatically fit the legs.

This feels much more premium and realistic.

---

### Overall rating

* **Current implementation:** **6.5/10**
* **With automatic alignment and snapping:** **8.5/10**
* **With body silhouettes/mannequins:** **9.5/10**

Given the direction of Ka.Sha, I think the silhouette/mannequin approach will provide a much more polished experience than a free-form drag-and-drop canvas. It will also make outfits look realistic and help customers visualize how different garments work together.
